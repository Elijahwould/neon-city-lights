# Levels and Ranks

As you advance and gain levels, each level has a title attached to it. Depending on the title, the MP cost of gaining a level will increase, and thus the amount of MP you require to reach the next rank will also increase. Below is a chart outlining character progression.

Example: If you're a level 13 Streetwise character on the standard track, it will cost you 5 MP to reach level 14. At level 14, you become a Shadowrunner, and your cost to level up increases to 6 MP.

*Leveling Costs & Ranks*

| 6 | Runt | 1 | 1 |
| 7 | Runt | 1 | 2 |
| 8 | Scrub | 3 | 3 |
| 9 | Scrub | 3 | 6 |
| 10 | Scrub | 3 | 9 |

# Why is Leveling Changing?

Right now, it takes roughly 19 games of obtaining max MP to get to max level on the server. That means that from levels 5 to 20, it takes you roughly right under a single job per level. It takes 3 games to exit Runt, 3 to exit Scrub, 3 to exit Streetwise, 4 to exit Shadowrunner, 4 to exit Vigilante, and 2 more to max out. Less games than there are levels. That's too fast! Characters barely get to have satisfying character arcs at this speed, and they will not be able to participate in many adventures in this way. It also means that people basically play their way out of being grouped up with other characters very, very quickly.

We also understand, however, that people want to get out of Runt to play more interesting games! Runt is great to help familiarize yourself with other players and the mechanics of the games. To that end, the progression adjustment changes the first three levels to go by one game at a time, before slowing things down.

It should **roughly** take 3 games to get out of Runt, 5 to get out of Scrub, 5 to get out of Streetwise, 6 to get out of Shadowrunner, 7 to get out of Vigilante, and 7 to 8 to max out. This increases the career of a character from 19 games to roughly 33—14 more games. This means that a character who gets regular games won't be leveling to max in under 20 weeks, which should give you way more time to develop your character in games—and keep you closer to certain level peer groups for a little longer.

Admittedly, a lot of people enjoy NCN because people feel they progress a lot quicker. This is still a lot faster than most Westmarch games, and certainly way faster than an in-home campaign, where long games can level up maybe once every four weeks! We hope this keeps leveling on a nice and smooth slope, with higher levels taking a little longer.

Having more characters is lower-level brackets is better for the overall health of the game, as well: more characters in these ranks means it's easier for fixers to overall fill groups for jobs at this level, which is good, because those games are less taxing to run for fixers, and they generally don't have characters that can solve problems with a snap of the fingers—and newer players will have more people to play with!
