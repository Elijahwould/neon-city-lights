Multiclassing allows you to gain levels in multiple classes. Doing so lets you mix the abilities of those classes to realize a character concept that might not be reflected in one of the standard class options. With this rule, you have the option of gaining a level in a new class whenever you advance in level, instead of gaining a level in your current class. Your levels in all your classes are added together to determine your character level.

For example, if you have three levels in Techwizard and two in Ronin, you’re a 5th-level character. As you advance in levels, you might primarily remain a member of your original class with just a few levels in another class, or you might change course entirely, never looking back at the class you left behind. You might even start progressing in a third or fourth class. Compared to a single-class character of the same level, you’ll sacrifice some focus in exchange for versatility.

## Experience Points

The experience point cost to gain a level is always based on your total character level, as shown in the Character Advancement table in chapter 1, not your level in a particular class. So, if you are an Ordinator 6/Ronin 1, you must gain enough XP to reach 8th level before you can take your second level as a Ronin or your seventh level as an Ordinator.

## Hit Points & Hit Dice

- You gain the hit points from your new class as described for levels after 1st.
- You gain the 1st-level hit points for a class only when you are a 1st-level character.
- You add together the Hit Dice granted by all your classes to form your pool of Hit Dice.
- If the Hit Dice are the same die type, you can simply pool them together.

For example, both the Ronin and the Enforcer have a d10, so if you are an Enforcer 5/Ronin 5, you have ten d10 Hit Dice. If your classes give you Hit Dice of different types, keep track of them separately. If you are an Enforcer 5/Ordinator 5, for example, you have five d10 Hit Dice and five d8 Hit Dice.

## Proficiency Bonus

Your proficiency bonus is always based on your total character level, as shown in the Character Advancement table in chapter 1, not your level in a particular class. For example, if you are a Ronin 3/Outlaw 2, you have the proficiency bonus of a 5th-level character, which is +3.

## Class Features

When you gain a new level in a class, you get its features for that level. A few features, however, have additional rules when you’re multiclassing: Extra Attack, Unarmored Defense, and Techcasting.

### Clause Perks & AI Assistance

You may be here going: what gives? Well, unlike Channel Divinity, which you may be more familiar with from, these are treated as two completely separate, discrete abilities, and items target them differently. Multiclassing treats these two abilities as fully separate as a result.

### Extra Attack

If you gain the Extra Attack class feature from more than one class, the features don’t add together. You can’t make more than two attacks with this feature unless it says you do (as the Ronin’s version of Extra Attack does). Similarly, the streamer’s [partnership program](/streamer:partnership-programs) Thirsting Blade doesn’t give you additional attacks if you also have Extra Attack.

### Unarmored Defence

If you already have the Unarmored Defense feature, you can't gain it again from another class.

### Techcasting

Your capacity for techcasting depends partly on your combined levels in all your techcasting classes and partly on your individual levels in those classes. Once you have the Techcasting feature from more than one class, use the rules below. If you multiclass but have the Techcasting feature from only one class, you follow the rules as described in that class.

You determine what techs you know and can prepare for each class individually, as if you were a single-classed member of that class. If you are a Auditor 4/Techwizard 3, for example, you know three 1st-level Auditor techs based on your levels in the Auditor class. As 3rd-level Techwizard, you know three Techwizard tricks, and your cyberdeck contains ten Techwizard techs, two of which (the two you gained when you reached 3rd level as a Techwizard) can be 2nd-level techs.

- If your Intelligence is 16, you can prepare six Techwizard techs from your cyberdeck.
- Each tech you know and prepare is associated with one of your classes, and you use the techcasting ability of that class when you cast the spell.
- Similarly, a techcasting focus, such as a holy symbol, can be used only for the techs from the class associated with that focus.

You determine your available tech slots by adding together all your levels in the Influencer, Ordinator, Shut-In, Source Coder, and Techwizard classes, half your levels (rounded down) in the Enforcer and Auditor classes, and a third of your Ronin or Outlaw levels (rounded down) if you have the Technoblade or the Hacktivist feature. Use this total to determine your tech slots by consulting the Multiclass Techcaster table.

- If you have more than one techcasting class, this table might give you tech slots of a level that is higher than the techs you know or can prepare.
- You can use those slots, but only to cast your lower-level techs.
- If a lower level tech that you cast, like *[flame vent](/tech:flame-vent)*, has an enhanced effect when cast using a higher-level slot, you can use the enhanced effect, even though you don’t have any techs of that higher level.

For example, if you are the aforementioned Auditor 4/Techwizard 3, you count as a 5th-level character when determining your tech slots: you have four 1st-level slots, three 2nd-level slots, and two 3rd-level slots. However, you don’t know any 3rd-level techs, nor do you know any 2nd-level Auditor techs. You can use the tech slots of those levels to cast the techs you do know and potentially enhance their effects.

If you have both the Techcasting class feature and the Gifted Tech class feature from the Streamer class, you can use the tech slots you gain from the Gifted Tech feature to cast techs you know or have prepared from classes with the Techcasting class feature, and you can use the tech slots you gain from the Techcasting class feature to cast Streamer techs you know.
