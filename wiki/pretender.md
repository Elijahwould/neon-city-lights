## "A lie told often enough becomes the truth."

##### Source: adapted from [Matthew Mercer's Blood Hunter (2022)](https://www.dndbeyond.com/classes/blood-hunter), Neon City NightsThanks to Quote_Traveler#1387 for the mechanic overhaul to Blood Hunter and reny#8234 for the job of adapting the flavor and slight balancing of the class to a version that fits our setting better!

*Frayed but resolute, its grimacing face dripping with sweat, an A.I.-Simar reddens a finger with its own wounds, then cannibalizes the nanites within to fuel deprecated augments foretelling a nascent obsession with decay. A surge of software and entropy twists through the air, breaching the DNA sequencing of a mutated behemoth to even the odds.*

*An attractive thulian dressed in an explosion of conflicting modern trends does a deep search at the site of an alleyway execution, her eyes flashing with recognition as she pieces together the grisly scene. Suddenly, she's certain in the knowledge of what kind of creature is responsible, and how best to mimic their splices.*

*Trawling through lightless tunnels between the cities, accompanied only by the distant degraded whine of ever-looping ads, the trash gremlin keys in to a looming malicious intent. She winces as she cashes in a few quick selfies with convenient product placement, the market influence mass-pinging her phone setting it aglow, eager to burn into the wallet of her enemies.*

Pretenders are clever mercs driven by a deep-seeded envy to both become and destroy that which most enthralls them. Armed with infusions of pirated experimental nanites and a willingness to betray their own morals and health for their falsehoods, they are equally as likely to defend the city from the shadows as they are to become a foreboding threat.

##### No Pain, No Gain

In the darkest of backalley deals, under the noses of every other interested party, pretenders have mastered the secretive techniques of nanophage, a natural evolution of nanotechs' prevalence in full biological integration, axed and defunded before even initial tests were finished. Through careful study and practice, pretenders hone the codestrings of nanophage into unique applications of violence, hyperintegrating new material to overcome their self-perceived crippling faults and compromising their truth to strike at foes with devastating sequence breaches.

##### Why Be You When You Can Be New

Whether driven by reverence for the accomplishments of eras forgotten, the need to remain popular, or the hope of being a better self to fit into an uncaring corpo-controlled area, every pretender has their own reasons for undertaking the first LiNKER screening that earns them a dose of nanophages. In donning a pretender's mask, one also joins an unknown community connected in their willingness to never be themselves. For many, this might be the only community they know, and in this kinship some pretenders create an identity that is felt deeper than any previously ascribed title of self.

Outside the cloistered communities they self-foster, however, the life of a pretender is a challenge. The initial LiNKER screening of the nanophage leaves one visibly changed, prone to attracting suspicion to those who might question the mask they've gained. Likewise, sequence breaches can spark security concerns in even the most well-read wiki admin. Pretenders feel most comfortable in disparate wastes, forgotten spire-alleys, and in isolation, the rare few places they don't have to worry about making an enemy if their mask were to slip. And it does slip. In pursuing the nanophage a pretender starts down a path of irrevocable self-alteration in the name of infatuation, trading physicality, emotion, and their morals for their goal, but the impetus to maintain down that path is a constant check on their willpower and determination.

# Class Features

As a pretender, you gain the following class features.

##### Hit Points

**Hit Dice:** 1d10 per pretender level

**Hit Points at 1st Level:** 10 + your Constitution modifier

**Hit Points at Higher Levels:** 1d10 (or 6) + your Constitution modifier per pretender level after 1st

##### Proficiencies

**Armor:** Light armor, medium armor, shields

**Weapons:** Civilian weapons, military weapons

**Tools:** Microlabs

**Saving Throws:** Dexterity, Intelligence

**Skills:** Choose three from Athletics, Acrobatics, Culture, History, Insight, Investigation, Survival, and Technology

##### Equipment

You start with the following equipment, in addition to the equipment granted by your background:

- (a) a military weapon or (b) two civilian weapons
- (a) a light pistol and 20 bullets or (b) a revolver and 20 bullets
- (a) micromesh armor or (b) nanoprene armor
- an edgerunner's pack

##### Multiclassing

The following contains information necessary for multiclassing as a Pretender.

**Ability Score Minimum.** You require both a Strength or Dexterity score and a Wisdom or Intelligence score of 13 or higher to multiclass into or out of this class.

If pretender isn't your initial class, you gain the following proficiencies when you take your first level as a pretender:

- **Armor.** Light armor, medium armor, shields
- **Weapons.** Civilian weapons, military weapons
- **Tools.** Microlabs
- **Gifted Tech.** If your pretender wears a mask of the PACT shill and also has streamer levels, add one-third of your pretender levels (rounded down) to your streamer level and consult the streamer progression table for total tech slots, tricks known, and tech slot level.

### LiNKER Screening

Beginning at 1st level, you have been screened for and dosed with LiNKER, a dangerous, one-of-a-kind secret drug compound that artificially induces a high sync rate between flesh and machine, instilling a certain instinct for technology. You have advantage on Wisdom (Survival) checks to track software-based creature types and hacked, as well as on Intelligence ability checks to recall information about them.

The LiNKER also unites your bodily senses to bear the brunt of instructing the nanophage, even if the burden might split the very seams of your body and mind. Some of your features require your target to make a saving throw to resist the feature's effects. The saving throw is calculated as follows, utilizing your Nanophage modifier, which is your choice of Wisdom or Intelligence (chosen when you gain this feature; if you choose Wisdom as your Nanophage modifier, you lose proficiency in Dexterity and Intelligence saving throws from this class, and instead gain proficiency in Strength and Wisdom saving throws):

**Nanophage save DC** = 8 + your proficiency bonus + your Nanophage modifier

### Biotech Sequencing

At 1st level, you gain the ability to direct, and sometimes overcharge, the nanophage fueling itself upon you to hyperanalyze and disrupt the connection between creatures and their own nanite colonies. You gain two [Sequence Breaches](/pretender:sequence-breaches) of your choice. You learn an additional Sequence Breach of your choice at 3rd level, and can choose a Sequence Breach you know and replace it with a different Sequence Breach. This occurs again at 6th, 11th, 15th, 18th, and 20th level as shown by the Pretender table.

These Sequence Breaches, among other features, are powered by Nanophage Dice. You have an amount of Nanophage Dice as shown on the Pretender class table, starting with 1.

You can execute a Sequence Breach by spending a Nanophage Die. When you execute a Sequence Breach, you can choose to cannibalize your own body to overcharge the Sequence Breach by rolling the expended Nanophage Die, according to the Nanophage Potency, and losing a number of hit points equal to the result. You must decide to cannibalize yourself for the breach before it affects the target. A cannibalized breach gains an additional effect, noted in its description.

You regain all Nanophage Dice at the end of a long rest.

### Lethal Error

At 2nd level, you learned how to induce a controlled external surge of your nanophage, at the expense of each sacrificed nanite lancing a crash report of pain through you. You learn a Hardware Error of your choice.

As a bonus action, you can spend a Nanophage Die to hyperlink a known error onto a single weapon. Roll that die, and lose hit points equal to the roll as you suffer the neurosynaptic crash report. Until the end of your next short or long rest, or if you are not holding the hyperlinked weapon at the end of your turn, attacks from this weapon are technical and deal additional damage equal to your Nanophage Potency. This additional damage is the same type as the error you hyperlinked to the weapon. If you are wielding two weapons, you may hyperlink both as part of the same bonus action, spending and rolling Nanophage Dice for each weapon. Other creatures can't gain the benefit of your errors.

A weapon can only support a single error at a time, with each end of a polearm counting as a separate weapon. The two ends do not need to be hyperlinked with the same error, and can both be hyperlinked at once, as two weapons.

You learn an additional Hardware Error at 7th level, and a single Theoretical Error at 11th.

### Fighting Style

You adopt a particular style of fighting as your specialty. Choose one of the following options. You can't take a FIghting Style option more than once, even if you later get to choose again.

- **Blind Fighting.** You have blindsight with a range of 10 feet. Within that range, you can effectively see anything that isn't behind total cover, even if you're blinded or in darkness. Moreover, you can see an invisible creature within that range, unless the creature successfully hides from you.

- **Clobbering Swing.** When you make a melee weapon attack roll with a weapon that deals bludgeoning damage, if you have advantage on the attack and your lower roll would hit a creature within 5 feet of the target of your attack, you can choose to deal damage to it equal to your Strength or Dexterity modifier, whichever is higher.

- **Dancing Thorns.** When you make a melee weapon attack roll with a weapon that deals piercing or slashing damage, if you have advantage on the attack and both attack rolls would have hit, you score a critical hit if either attack is only 1 off from naturally scoring a critical hit.

- **Dueling.** When you are wielding a melee weapon in one hand and no other weapons, you gain a +2 bonus to damage rolls with that weapon.

- **Great Weapon Fighting.** When you roll a 1 or 2 on a damage die for an attack you make with a melee weapon that you are wielding with two hands, you can reroll the die and must use the new roll, even if the new roll is a 1 or a 2. The weapon must have the two-handed or versatile property for you to gain this benefit.

- **Gun-Fu.** When making a one-handed ranged attack against an opponent within melee reach of you, you do not have disadvantage on the attack roll. Your ranged attacks ignore half cover and three-quarters cover against targets within melee reach of you. You gain a +1 bonus to attack rolls on ranged attacks made with one-handed ranged weapons.

- **Marksmanship.** You gain a +2 bonus to attack rolls you make with two-handed ranged weapons.

- **Riot Fighting.** As a bonus action, you can enter a defensive stance that lasts until the start of your next turn. While in your defensive stance, you can make opportunity attacks without using your reaction, and you can use your reaction to make a melee attack against a creature that moves more than 5 feet while within your reach.

- **Thrown Weapon Fighting.** You can draw a weapon that has the thrown property as part of the attack you make with the weapon.
  - In addition, when you hit with a ranged attack using a thrown weapon, you gain a +2 bonus to the damage roll.

- **Two-Weapon Fighting.** When you engage in two-weapon fighting, you can add your ability modifier to the damage of the second attack.

- **Unarmed Fighting.** Your unarmed strikes can deal bludgeoning damage equal to 1d6 + your Strength modifier on a hit. If you aren't wielding any weapons or a shield when you make the attack roll, the d6 becomes a d8.
  - At the start of each of your turns, you can deal 1d4 bludgeoning damage to one creature grappled by you.

### Pretender's Mask

At 3rd level, you commit yourself to the imitation of a dedicated archetype. The mask you choose grants you features at 3rd, 7th, 11th, 15th, and 18th level.

### Ability Score Improvement

When you reach 4th level, and again at 8th, 12th, 16th, and 19th level, you can increase one ability score of your choice by 2, or you can increase two ability scores of your choice by 1. As normal, you can't increase an ability score above 20 using this feature.

### Extra Attack

Beginning at 5th level, you can attack twice, instead of once, whenever you take the Attack action on your turn.

### Retributive Monitor Chip

At 6th level, when you damage a creature using your Lethal Error feature, you can choose to condense your nanophage into a tracking chit within them. You always know the direction to the chipped creature, and once per round, when it deals damage to you or a creature you can see within 5 feet of you, the chipped creature takes software damage equal to your Nanophage modifier.

This chip remains active until you deactivate it (no action required), or apply a chip to another creature. Your chip can be deactivated with *[localized EMP](/tech:localized-emp)*, and is treated as a tech with a level equal to half your pretender level (maximum 9th level).

You can use this feature once, regaining use of it on a short or long rest. You gain an additional use of this feature at 12th level.

### Seen Some Shit

When you reach 9th level, your ever-present paranoia of being caught up in your lies has scarred every suspicious sight into the back of your mind. When making an Intelligence (History) check to recall information about a dark or circumspect past surrounding an object you are touching, or a location you are present in, you have advantage on the roll. The information gleaned often leans towards the sinister or conspiratorial influences of the past, and sometimes conveys information previously unknown to the character on higher rolls at the fixer's discretion, through stress-induced visions and crackpot theory breakthroughs.

### LiNKER ADY-Refinement

Upon reaching 10th level, you are called in for a second LiNKER screening, a new, personalized, dosage permanently allowing you to reinforce yourself and further integrate with your nanophage colony. Your speed increases by 10 feet, you gain nightvision out to 60 feet, if you already have nightvision, extend it by an additional 30 feet. Whenever you make a Strength, Dexterity, or Constitution saving throw, you can spend and roll a Nanophage Die, reducing your hit points by the result of the roll, to instead make a saving throw with your Nanophage ability score.

### Monitor Chip Upgrade: Lockdown

Starting at 13th level, you can maintain wireless access with the chips manifested of your nanophage and enact a direct denial of service. The software damage from your Retributive Monitor Chip increases to twice your Nanophage modifier (minimum of 2).

In addition, a chipped creature cannot take the Dash action, and if a creature chipped by you attempts to transmit or leave its local realm via ability, tech, gate, or digital tool, it takes 4d6 software damage and must make a Wisdom saving throw. On a failure, the attempt to leave its current realm fails.

### Tired and Jaded

When you reach 14th level, the shit you've seen has numbed you to attempts of piercing your guise, whether they be to shake or entice you. You have advantage on saving throws against being charmed and frightened.

### So That's As Far As You Can Go

At 17th level, you refuse to let your nanophage alone choose the limits of your self-deception and push the absolute limit of risk and reward. Once per turn, when you would spend a Nanophage Die, you can instead spend four hit dice. If you would roll the Nanophage Dice, instead roll all four hit dice, losing hit points equal to the total roll.

### Perfect Synchronization

At 20th level, the pain of your nanophage has numbed, it's unconscious as breathing. There is no longer any identifiable division between what is you, nanite, and constituent parts. While your hit points are below half your hit point maximum, the damage die for your Lethal Errors becomes d12s. While your hit points are below one fourth your hit point maximum, the damage rolled for your Lethal Errors is maximized.

Additionally, you add half your Constitution modifier (minimum of 1) to death saving throws. Once per short or long rest, if this bonus would increase the roll to 20 or higher, you can treat it as if you had rolled a 20 on the die.
