## "What is a man? A miserable little pile of secrets. But enough talk… Have at you!"

The legends of vampirism have been passed down in story since humans learned to fear the dark. Finding the figures of those myths walking before them under another name, the pretenders of the mask of the sipping strain sought to join their ranks, while perhaps circumventing the user permission restrictions of the ransomware that is the internet sucking viral strain. They have achieved a fugue state, just short of any strain family or immortality in hardware. Their nanophage has been interwoven with datapackets and corrupted programs that have grazed proper strains, but they fall just short of proper progeny, tortuously untainted. It's morbin' time.

### Hacked-Humanoid Interface

When you don this mask at 3rd level, you piece together and integrate out-of-date prosthesis to channel bodily augmentations in the image of netsuckers. You gain the following benefit:

- **Colorless Claws and Fangs.** Your custom-modeled jaw and sharp, mechanical, claws are natural weapons. You can use Dexterity instead of Strength for the attack and damage rolls with them. These attacks deal 1d6 piercing damage, increasing to 1d8 at 11th level, and 1d10 at 18th level. You can hyperlink your claws and fangs with a Lethal Error as a single weapon.

Additionally, your Lethal Error feature improves, and you learn the following error.

| Mask Error | Damage Type |
| --- | --- |
| LifeSprtErr.CELLDECAY | Entropic |

### Nanoransom Sequencing

Also at 3rd level, your Sequence Breaches incorporate a simulation of the injection and biopaywall programs that form the basis of a 'sucker's bite. When you would cannibalize yourself for a Sequence Breach, you can choose for your nanophage to forcibly integrate the nanites they disrupt. Instead of losing hit points equal to your Nanophage Potency, you inflict the result as damage to the target of your Sequence Breach, the damage type being chosen from the Lethal Errors you know. You then regain hit points equal to the damage dealt.

You can ransom your Sequence Breaches a number of times equal to your Nanophage modifier (minimum of 1), regaining all uses on a long rest.

### Hackjob Flitter

At 7th level, you've hobbled together the hardware to traverse the air and maneuver the battlefield with ease. As a bonus action, you can activate your hackjob, possibly going partially digital or growing hardlight wings in reverence of a proper strain, or deploying joint-mounted jets to make your own of it, gaining a flying speed equal to your walking speed. By expending a Nanophage Die, rolling it and reducing your hit points by the total amount, you can activate this flight as a reaction, and immediately move up to half your speed without triggering opportunity attacks. This flight lasts for 1 hour. At the end of the duration, you slowly fall back down to the ground at a safe speed.

You can use this ability once per short or long rest. You gain an additional use of this feature at 15th level.

### Monitor Chip Upgrade: Autodrain

At 11th level, your Retributive Monitor Chip can act as a microtized manufactorum, eating away at your victims for substance. As a bonus action, you can cause a creature chipped by you within 60 feet to lose hit points equal to two rolls of your Nanophage Potency. You then heal for half the result.

### Biotech Nanovoracity

At 15th level, your mastery of your faux-ransomware expands. Whenever one of your Sequence Breaches ends on a hostile creature, you can choose to draw their nanites into their blood as your nanophage retreats, dealing entropic damage to them equal to your Pretender level.

In addition, once per turn, when you kill a creature that has blood, you can capitalize on their hyperical state and harvest nanites from their exsanguination, regaining hit points equal to your Pretender level.

### Absolute Hemosync

At 18th level, the mask has become the face, and your nanomanipulative abilities culminate in total hemotechnical control. When you would regain hit points as a result of your Nanoransom Sequencing, Monitor Chip Upgrade: Autodrain, or Biotech Nanovoracity features, you can choose to forgo healing to instead gain a charge of Hemotech.

You can have a number of charges of Hemotech equal to your Nanophage modifier (minimum of 1), and whenever you finish a long rest, you lose all Hemotech charges.

When you make an attack roll, ability check, or saving throw, you can choose to expend one charge of Hemotech to add 1d6 to your roll. You can do so after rolling, but before you know the result of the roll.

Additionally, as a bonus action, you can spend Hemotech charges to regain hit points. You roll Nanophage Potency for each charge spent in this way, regaining hit points equal to the total result plus your Constitution modifier.
