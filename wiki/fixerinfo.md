# What is a Fixer?

A Fixer is a person who volunteers to run jobs on the server—basically, a dungeon master who runs quests/adventures. Fixers get certain benefits for participating in this manner: as they progress in rank, they can have more direct impact on world lore (within reason), they gain an additional character and townie slot, and gain notoriety each time they run a game.

# Fixer Ranks and Guidelines

There are three ranks of Fixer on the server:

- **Mini Fixer.** This is a person who has just applied to be a fixer. They have certain restrictions on the type of game they can run and how to run it, but receive regular notoriety rewards.
  - Mini Fixers must have their jobs approved before they can run them. Submit a job for approval by using the Gig Post Format later in this page and posting it in #trainee-approvals, and create a thread attached to the post. People may ask questions and clarification before giving approval.
  - The Mini Fixer needs to run 3 passing games before they are promoted to Full Fixer. A Full Fixer or Trusted Fixer needs to be around to watch the game as it unfolds (and to intercede if necessary), and they are the fixer that will give a passing or failing grade. The Mini Fixer must secure a Full or Trusted Fixer to watch the game before they run it. A Lore Team member can substitute if available and willing.
  - Mini Fixer jobs cannot kill runners. A runner that dies can be removed from the scene, but the fixer should give a roleplay reason as to why the runner either survives or is returned to life.
  - Mini Fixer jobs cannot have world-altering consequences. Feel free to get your observing Fixer to intercede if necessary.
  - Mini Fixers cannot respond to Player Requests.
- **Full Fixer.** This is a person who has passed at least 3 games and is experienced enough to no longer require observation. They receive regular notoriety rewards for running games, and also have one additional character slot and one additional townie slot.
  - Full Fixers do not generally need their jobs approved before they can run them. Some exceptions exist: they still need to ask for permission to use certain corps, and anything that might alter world lore requires approval.
  - Full Fixers can respond to Player Requests.
- **Trusted Fixer.** This is a person who has run many games after becoming a Full Fixer who is trusted by the Lore team to maintain a high standard of quality in their games and consistently getting positive reviews in feedback. They receive the same rewards as Full Fixers.
  - Trusted Fixers have a lot more latitude when it comes to the kind of jobs they can run in terms of lore impact, though they still should iron out all details with the Lore team.
  - Trusted Fixers can respond to Player Requests.

# Fixer Rewards

## Flat Gains

All Full and Trusted Fixers gain the following, permanently:

- An additional character slot (up to 4, or 5 if the fixer has a character ranked Streetwise or higher)
- An additional townie slot (up to 2)

## Base Notoriety Gain

Fixers gain [notoriety](/notoriety) for running games. The amount is calculated as followed:

- You gain notoriety based on the **lowest allowed rank** of the job you run, using the following chart:
  - **Runt.** 20 base notoriety (levels 5-7).
  - **Scrub.** 40 base notoriety (levels 8-10).
  - **Streetwise.** 60 base notoriety (levels 11-13).
  - **Shadowrunnner.** 70 base notoriety (levels 14-16).
  - **Vigilante and above.** 80 base notoriety (levels 17-20).
  - **Example.** If you ran a job with four players and the lowest level player was Scrub, you gain **40** notoriety.

## Bonus Notoriety Gain

You will be given a bonus for posting your job at least **12 hours** before game start.

- If you announce your job 12+ hours in advance, you gain **5** bonus notoriety upon posting the completion log.
- If instead you announce your job 24+ hours in advance, you gain **10** bonus notoriety upon posting the completion log.

You will be given a bonus for **selecting players** in specific ways.

- If at least half (but not all) of the players in the game were either priority selected or randomly selected, you gain **5** bonus notoriety upon posting the completion log.
- If instead all of the players in the game were either priority selected or randomly selected, you gain **10** bonus notoriety upon posting the completion log.

You will be given a bonus when the job is meant to **fulfill a player request** from the #player-requests channel.

- **Runt.** +5 bonus notoriety (levels 5-7).
- **Scrub.** +10 bonus notoriety (levels 8-10).
- **Streetwise.** +20 bonus notoriety (levels 11-13).
- **Shadowrunnner.** +30 bonus notoriety (levels 14-16).
- **Vigilante and above.** +40 bonus notoriety (levels 17-20).

You will be given a bonus when the job is a **multi-part mission**, beginning on part 2. Note that this bonus does not stack with request fulfilment. However you will gain an additional 10 notoriety for each request fulfilment that meets the multi-part mission criteria.

- **Runt.** +10 bonus notoriety (levels 5-7).
- **Scrub.** +15 bonus notoriety (levels 8-10).
- **Streetwise.** +25 bonus notoriety (levels 11-13).
- **Shadowrunnner.** +35 bonus notoriety (levels 14-16).
- **Vigilante and above.** +45 bonus notoriety (levels 17-20).

# Expected Reward Guidelines

## Expected Rewards Per Rank

These are not hard limits, but this resolves many issues with low-rank games leveling players too quickly. Players should expect to not blow past the lower levels like they seem to have been doing. The lower levels of a character's development are actually quite important! Runt games should not be long, massive, epic affairs unless everyone is really interested in that: they should be the time where people get a feel for their character and start to make connections, so having many games they can play in where they can meet a bunch of different characters is important, to build in-character rapport.

- **Runt.** 1 MP max base, up to 2x rank credits.
- **Scrub.** 2 MP max base, up to 3x rank credits.
- **Streetwise.** 3 MP max base, up to 4x rank credits.
- **Shadowrunner and above.** 3 MP max base, up to 5x rank credits.

No game should realistically give more than 3 MP, and games should be granting more MP based on the difficulty of the game. More difficult games should run longer.

Games that may go over a day of playtime may consider splitting into a **Part 2** or multi-part game.

Difficulty is not always measured in combat; many games that have complicated roleplaying scenarios, advanced skill check scenarios, clever use of resources (such as spell slots, credits, consumable items, planning, etc.) and more should absolutely count as difficult games. Combat is not the end-all, be-all of tabletop roleplaying games!

## Notoriety as a Reward

You can never grant [notoriety](/notoriety) for jobs, save under very specific circumstances:

1. The job is a ***World Event*** job, approved by the Lore Team and Trusted Fixers.
2. The person being granted notoriety in the job pulled partial DMing duty (such as a player slipping in to help co-DM a part of the game).
3. One of the player characters dies during the mission, and the death is permanent—you can give Notoriety to the player equal to 10 times the MP they would've gotten.

Notoriety is almost explicitly meant for contributing to the community. Players can gain Notoriety by running games as a fixer, submitting lore, or submitting mechanics changes or features that get accepted into the game.

## Item Rewards

If you intend on giving an item to a player, make sure that you are falling within the expected rewards for their level by checking the [Leveling and Rewards](/leveling-and-rewards) page and comparing the item cost. Item costs are color-coded in the [spreadsheet for the Neon City Grey Market](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/u/4/d/1tDmwyNPJhtff8eAwCRvCqd1u_X6VwggSg-IuXeEYEMg/edit#gid=0), which should help you quickly look up an item you can get.

# Gig Post Format

When posting a gig in [#job-forum-listings](https://discord.com/channels/931248195105869905/931321351594201138), you can copy and paste the below format/template and use it to save time (and make it look nice)!

## Templates

```
**GIG NAME:** Gig Name
**GIG ALIGNMENT:** Alignments allowed (Corpo, Freelancer, Rebel)
**GIG LEVEL:** Level range, with @ tag
**GIG LOCATION:** Channel, usually #job-posting-X
**GIG TIME:** Time and date of gig, use https://r.3v.fi/discord-timestamps/
**GIG RUNTIME:** How long is the gig expected to run?
**GIG THEME:** What the gig's about (combat, infiltration, stealth, roleplay, social, etc.)
**GIG WARNINGS:** Content warnings for the players (such as body horror, dark themes, etc.)
**GIG HOOK:** A job description. You can use a code block for more stuff, like so:
```diff
- RED COLOR TITLE »

You can write stuff here too.
+ Start with a plus sign for green text.```
**PLAYERS:** Amount of players!
**LEWD LEVEL RATING:** LL0, 1, 2 or 3
**KINKS LIKELY:** What fun things can people expect in an LL1+ game?
**KINKS PROHIBITED:** What things will you not do, nor do you want players to engage in?
**HOW TO JOIN:** How can people join your game? Usually, it's a react + a reply in a thread.
```
