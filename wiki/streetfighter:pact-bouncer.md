## "I'm sorry, buddy, but it seems to me like you've had a bit too much to drink."

Sometimes, a street fighter feels they deserve a cut of the fame and fortune they can't earn in back alleys. Sometimes, a street fighter is more concerned with keeping their pockets full of ill-gotten ad revenue and their gizmos up to date than with who they're kicking the shit out of. These morally dubious lot are the muscular backstagemen and door blockers to a streamer's pretty face, the hired stiffs of Partnered Autonomous Commercial Title sponsor groups.

##### Source: adapted from [Benjamin Huffman's Pugilist v3](https://www.dmsguild.com/product/184921/The-Pugilist-Class)

### "Donated" Tech

When you choose this discipline at 3rd level, you learn the charged railgun shot and nanotech twiddling tricks. Constitution is your techcasting ability for these techs, since your oh-so-generous benefactors petered out your gift through dubiously hidden nanite infusions and hardlight exposure.

### The Beat Stick

Also starting at 3rd level, you can use a bonus action on your turn to purge your body of excessive PACT-branded nano or holo tech. When you do, one of your limbs (often an arm) is technologically overlayed with a brand-appropriate yet intimidating translucent visage of power for 1 minute (a tentacle for HentaiHub, a dark claw for Goth Topic, etc.), granting you the following benefits:

- When you roll a 1 on a damage die for an unarmed strike, you can reroll the die and must use the new roll, even if the new roll is a 1.
- The first time you miss with an unarmed strike each turn, you can make an additional unarmed strike as part of the same action.
- Immediately after you take the Attack action on your turn, you can spend 2 mettle points to make three unarmed strikes as a bonus action.

You must finish a short or long rest before you can use this feature again.

### PACT Rewards Programs

Starting at 6th level, you gain two sponsorship programs of your choice from among those available to the streamer class. For every two levels you have in this class, you count as possessing one streamer, as if you were a streamer, for the purpose of meeting a sponsorship program or feat's prerequisites. Constitution is your techcasting ability for any techs you gain as a result of one of these sponsorship programs, and any sponsorship program gained this way that references your Charisma modifier uses your Strength modifier instead.

When you gain a level in this class, you can request a new loyalty program perk, choosing one of the programs you know and replacing it with another program that you meet the prerequisites for.

### The Hired Muscle

Starting at 11th level, when you use the beat stick feature, you can choose to encase yourself entirely, growing into a real billboard of a blockade for an equal amount of time. When you do, your size increases by one category—from Medium to Large, for example—if there is enough room to support such growth. Regardless of if you change sizes, while this feature is active, you have advantage on Strength checks and Strength saving throws, your reach becomes 10 feet, and your melee weapon attacks deal 1d4 extra damage.

When the feature ends, you suffer one level of exhaustion.

### Use of Excessive Force

Starting at 17th level, you can use an action and expend 6 mettle points to attempt to escort a rowdy customer outside of the establishment of life, utilizing your "gifts" to burrow your hand into one target creature within your reach's body and extracting its innards. The creature must make a Dexterity saving throw (DC equal to 8 + your proficiency bonus + your Strength modifier). On a failure, the creature takes 100 technical piercing damage. On a success, the creature takes 50 technical piercing damage instead.

If this damage reduces the target to 0 hit points or less, it immediately dies as you rip a hole through it, creating a censor-earning fountain of viscera. When this happens, each creature within 30 feet of you who can see you must succeed on a Wisdom saving throw against the same DC or be frightened of you for 1 minute. A creature who fails the saving throw can make the Wisdom saving throw again at the end of each of its turns, ending the frightened condition early on a success.

Once you use this feature, you must finish a long rest before you can use it again.
