Conditions alter a creature’s capabilities in a variety of ways and can arise as a result of a power, a class feature, a monster’s attack, or other effect. Most conditions, such as blinded, are impairments, but a few, such as invisible, can be advantageous.

A condition lasts either until it is countered (the prone condition is countered by standing up, for example) or for a duration specified by the effect that imposed the condition.

If multiple effects impose the same condition on a creature, each instance of the condition has its own duration, but the condition’s effects don’t get worse. A creature either has a condition or doesn’t.

The following definitions specify what happens to a creature while it is subjected to a condition.

### [Blinded](/con:blinded)

Your eyes have been stained, your cameras fizzled, your vision obscured.

- A blinded creature can’t see and automatically fails any ability check that requires sight.
- Attack rolls against the creature have advantage, and the creature’s attack rolls have disadvantage, unless a sense or ability states otherwise.

### [Charmed](/con:charmed)

Your vitals stutter and at the back of your mind you recognize a friend, how silly of you.

- A charmed creature can’t attack the charmer or target the charmer with harmful abilities or effects.
- The charmer has advantage on any ability check to interact socially with the creature.

### [Deafened](/con:deafened)

Your ears are ringing, your audio channels have peaked, your world mute.

- A deafened creature can’t hear and automatically fails any ability check that requires hearing.

### [Doomed](/con:doomed)

You've run afoul of the wrong CEO, an esoteric ~ATH code, or even your own body. You aren't long for this world.

- A doomed creature dies after 13 (2d12) hours have passed after gaining the condition, or at a greater amount of time as determined by the fixer.
- A doomed creature continues to be doomed even after it dies. Effects equivalent to a 7th-level or higher tech can remove the doomed condition (such as *[repair](/tech:repair)* cast on a living creature, *[remodel](/tech:remodel)*, *[total recreation](/tech:total-recreation)*, or *[absolute source code manipulation](/tech:absolute-source-code-manipulation)*).

### [Exhaustion](/con:exhaustion)

Your head is light yet your limbs are stone, your mind is racing but thoughts are fleeting, you might even have a foot in the grave.

*Exhaustion Level*

| Level | Fatigue; Physical Effect | Stress; Mental Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Disadvantage on Strength, Dexterity, and Constitution checks | Disadvantage on Intelligence, Wisdom, and Charisma checks |
| 2 | Speed halved | Can take an action or a bonus action each turn, not both |
| 3 | Disadvantage on attack rolls and saving throws using Strength, Dexterity, or Constitution | Disadvantage on attack rolls and saving throws using Intelligence, Wisdom, or Charisma |
| 4 | Hit point maximum halved | Suffer the effects of a randomly- or fixer-determined minor derangement |
| 5 | Speed reduced to 5 feet | Cannot cast leveled techs |
| 6 | [Doomed](/con:doomed) | Suffer the effects of a randomly- or fixer-determined major derangement |

Some effects, such as extremely hazardous terrain, poignant anguish, or specific technical devices, can cause exhaustion. Exhaustion is measured in six, differentiated levels, separated by physical or mental exhaustion effects, called fatigue and stress. An effect can give a creature one or both variations of exhaustion, or multiple levels at once, as specified in the effect's description.

If an already exhausted creature suffers another effect that causes exhaustion, its current exhaustion level increases by the amount specified in the effect’s description, unless the effect causes specifically a level of fatigue when the creature has only the equal level of stress, or vice versa, in which cause it simply gains the other exhaustion effect of that level.

When a creature suffers either or both effects of its current exhaustion level it also suffers from all lower levels. For example, a creature suffering level 2 fatigue has both level 1 fatigue and stress, even if they only had one or the other before going to exhaustion level 2.

An effect that removes exhaustion reduces its level as specified in the effect’s description, clearing both fatigue and stress of the appropriate level, with all exhaustion effects ending if a creature’s exhaustion level is reduced below 1.

Finishing a long rest reduces a creature's exhaustion level by 1, provided that the creature has met their need for sustenance, or lack thereof. Also, being raised from the dead reduces a creature’s exhaustion level by 1.

### [Frightened](/con:frightened)

A shiver runs up your spine and ice rushes through your veins, fuck fight, freeze, or flirt, *run.*

- A frightened creature has disadvantage on ability checks and attack rolls while it is able to see the source of its fear.
- A frightened creature can’t willingly move closer to the source of its fear.

### [Grappled](/con:grappled)

A strong hand on the scruff like a lost kitten, a lasso of nanites, any manner of snare summed up as: you got got!

- A grappled creature's speed becomes 0 and it can't benefit from bonuses to movement speeds.
- The condition ends if the grappler is [incapacitated](/con:incapacitated).
- The condition also ends if an effect removes the grappled creature from the reach of the grappler or grappling effect, such as when a creature is hurled away by the *[shockwave](/tech:shockwave)* tech.

### [Headache](/con:headache)

There's a pressure around your head that makes thinking things through… harder.

- A creature suffering a headache subtracts half its proficiency bonus from any check or save using its Intelligence, Wisdom, or Charisma score.
- Any effect that would cure the [poisoned](/con:poisoned) condition cures a headache as well.
- Any effect that grants advantage against or immunity to the [poisoned](/con:poisoned) condition does not apply to a headache.

### [Incapacitated](/con:incapacitated)

It's kind of like spacing out and forgetting to breathe for a few seconds, but as a result of violence.

- An incapacitated creature can’t take actions or reactions.

### [Inflammation](/con:inflammation)

There's a locking of your joints and stiffness of bloat throughout your body that makes moving… difficult.

- A creature suffering inflammation subtracts half its proficiency bonus from any check or save using its Strength, Dexterity, or Constitution score.
- Any effect that would cure the [poisoned](/con:poisoned) condition cures inflammation as well.
- Any effect that grants advantage against or immunity to the [poisoned](/con:poisoned) condition does not apply to inflammation.

### [Invisible](/con:invisible)

Typically achieved through light refraction or extreme environmental mimicry. Either way, *poof*, gone.

- An invisible creature is impossible to see without the aid of tech or specific senses.
- An invisible creature is heavily obscured for the purpose of hiding.
- An invisible creature's location can be detected by any noise it makes or tracks it leaves.
- Attack rolls made by an invisible creature are made with advantage, unless the target can see through the invisibility.
- Attack rolls against an invisible creature are made with disadvantage, unless the attacker can see through the invisibility.

### [Irradiated](/con:irradiated)

The things that make you *you* are being torn apart at the seams, but surely you'll be just fine!

*Irradiation Level*

| Level | Decay; Living Effect | Interference; Automated Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Maximum hit points reduced by irradiation level every hour | All damage dealt reduced by irradiation level |
| 2 | [Poisoned](/con:poisoned) |  |
| 3 | All damage taken increased by irradiation level | AC reduced by irradiation level |
| 4 | Penalty to a randomly-determined ability score equal to irradiation level |  |
| 5 | Cannot receive restoration from any tech of lower level than irradiation level | [Blinded](/con:blinded) |
| 6 | [Doomed](/con:doomed) |  |

Some effects, such as areas of lasting fallout, radioactive materials, or specific technical devices, can cause irradiation. Irradiation is measured in six levels. Some levels are differentiated by whether they impact technology or biology, denoted as interference and decay, respectively. Interference applies to any creature that is or would read as a software creature type, hacked, or machine. Decay applies to all other creatures. Creatures that are both creature types suffer from both effects. An effect can give a creature one or more levels of irradiation as specified in the effect's description.

When irradiation level would cause a creature to be subject to an effect they would normally be immune to, they are not immune to that instance of the condition. A creature that would be immune to or have advantage against a certain condition imposed at a certain level of irradiation would enjoy advantage against that level of irradiation if it grants a save.

If an already irradiated creature suffers another effect that causes irradiation, its current irradiation level increases by the amount specified in the effect’s description.

A creature suffers the effect of its current irradiation level as well as all lower levels. For example, a creature suffering level 2 irradiation is poisoned and has its hit points or damage reduced as appropriate to its apparent creature type.

An effect that removes irradiation reduces its level as specified in the effect’s description, with all irradiation effects ending if a creature’s irradiation level is reduced below 1, however, when a creature is cured of radiation, roll a d20. On a roll of 1, they continue to suffer from the penalties of the highest level of radiation they had until subject to a *[primary system flush](/tech:primary-system-flush)*.

Finishing a long rest reduces a creature's irradiation level by 1, provided that the creature spends said rest in a life-promoting environment, such as one generated by a sense tank or the *[sanctify](/tech:sanctify)* tech. Also, being raised from the dead reduces a creature’s irradiation level by 1.

### [Paralyzed](/con:paralyzed)

Imagine everything that sucks about being [stunned](/con:stunned) and now imagine it's worse.

- A paralyzed creature is [incapacitated](/con:incapacitated) and can’t move or speak.
- The creature automatically fails Strength and Dexterity saving throws.
- Attack rolls against a paralyzed creature have advantage.
- Any attack that hits the creature is a critical hit if the attacker is within 5 feet of the creature.

### [Petrified](/con:petrified)

Your animation has been revoked by a substance, like metal, glass, or stone. Not what we meant by rock hard.

- A petrified creature is transformed, along with any generic object it is wearing or carrying, into a solid inanimate substance.
- A petrified creature's weight increases by a factor of ten, and it ceases aging.
- A petrified creature is [incapacitated](/con:incapacitated), can’t move or speak, and is unaware of its surroundings.
- Attack rolls against a petrified creature have advantage.
- A petrified creature automatically fails Strength and Dexterity saving throws.
- A petrified creature has resistance to all damage.
- A petrified creature is immune to poison and disease, although a poison or disease already in its system is suspended, not neutralized.

### [Poisoned](/con:poisoned)

Contracting a proper biohazardous affliction is rare in the age of neon, but sources are numerous.

- A poisoned creature has disadvantage on attack rolls and ability checks.

### [Prone](/con:prone)

People say "sweep the legs" isn't a valid technique, but consider, *think fast chucklenuts!* And now you're on your ass.

- A prone creature’s only movement option is to crawl, every 1 foot of movement costing 1 extra foot, until it stands up.
- A prone creature can stand up by using half of its movement.
- A prone creature makes melee attack rolls with disadvantage.
- An attack roll against a prone creature has advantage if the attacker is within 5 feet of the creature. Otherwise, the attack roll has disadvantage.

### [Rattled](/con:rattled)

Imagine everything that sucks about being [stunned](/con:stunned) and now imagine it isn't actually that bad.

- A rattled creature can’t take reactions.
- A rattled creature suffers a penalty equal to half their proficiency bonus to any check that adds their proficiency bonus. Effects that allow a rattled creature to add their proficiency bonus twice suffer the penalty twice.
- Any effect that would cure the [stunned](/con:stunned) condition cures rattled as well.
- Any effect that grants advantage against or immunity to the [stunned](/con:stunned) condition applies to rattled as well.

### [Restrained](/con:restrained)

This goes beyond getting got, you've been peach dropped, pinned, and tied up at this point.

- A restrained creature's speed becomes 0 and it can't benefit from bonuses to movement speeds.
- A restrained creature makes melee attack rolls with disadvantage.
- Attack rolls against a restrained creature are made with advantage.
- A restrained creature has disadvantage on Dexterity saving throws.

### [Stunned](/con:stunned)

Your nerves pinch and your body stops. Maybe you just got your dome blasted, but more likely, you got your chakras blitzed.

- A stunned creature is [incapacitated](/con:incapacitated), can’t move, and can speak only falteringly.
- A stunned creature automatically fails Strength and Dexterity saving throws.
- Attack rolls against a stunned creature have advantage.

### [Stupefied](/con:stupefied)

Having good ideas and using big words is for more intelli… Entelly… Smarterer people than you!

- A stupefied creature takes a -1 penalty to its AC, and d20 roll using its Wisdom or Intelligence score, and any DC generated using its Wisdom or Intelligence score.
- A stupefied creature's speed is reduced by 5 feet and it can not take the Dash action.
- A stupefied creature can speak only in simplified or stereotypically 'dumb' language.
- A stupefied creature has difficulty with concepts such as resisting temptation, complex calculation, and self-preservation.

### [Unconscious](/con:unconscious)

Turn all the brains and computers off for a bit, mm, okay, night-night…

- An unconscious creature is [incapacitated](/con:incapacitated), can’t move or speak, and is unaware of its surroundings
- An unconscious creature drops whatever it’s holding and falls [prone](/con:prone).
- An unconscious creature automatically fails Strength and Dexterity saving throws.
- Attack rolls against an unconscious creature have advantage.
- Any attack that hits the creature is a critical hit if the attacker is within 5 feet of the creature.
