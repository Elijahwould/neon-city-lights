Street Fighters are experts in bringing opponents to their knees through the proper application of physical force. While lacking the CHI implants of their brawler allies, they more than make up for it with their focus and sheer determination. It can be easy to force one to their knees—the trick, though, is keeping them there.

##### An Unstoppable Force

In a fight, it's often not about how hard you hit—but about how hard you can get hit and keep moving forward. Street Fighters embody this mentality well, often outlasting others through sheer force of will. For many, this takes on different shapes. But across the spectrum of those who carry the name, all of them can often find a way to stay standing just that little bit longer to achieve their goals.

##### An Immovable Object

Those who call themselves Street Fighters are many, but the number that truly are such are few. They often can be found taking jobs that entail physical combat, allowing them to hone their skills, or chase an adrenaline rush. Some are corporate sponsored pit fighters, breaking noses in the ring while repping their benefactors; others are rebellious vigilante investigators, hunting down those they believe have wronged them. What unites them all, however, is when the stakes are down and the bell has rung, they are always ready to step up and test their mettle.

# Class Features

As a street fighter, you gain the following class features.

##### Hit Points

**Hit Dice:** 1d8 per street fighter level

**Hit Points at 1st Level:** 8 + your Constitution modifier

**Hit Points at Higher Levels:** 1d8 (or 5) + your Constitution modifier per street fighter level after 1st

##### Proficiencies

**Armor:** Light armor

**Weapons:** Civilian weapons, improvised weapons, whip, revolver

**Tools:** Your choice of one vehicle, gaming set, or locksmithing kit

**Saving Throws:** Strength, Constitution

**Skills:** Choose two from Acrobatics, Athletics, Deception, Intimidation, Perception, Sleight of Hand, and Stealth

##### Equipment

You start with the following equipment, in addition to the equipment granted by your upbringing:

- (a) nanofilm armor or (b) any civilian weapon
- (a) an edgerunner’s pack or (b) a hobo's pack
- (a) a street scooter or (b) a gaming set or (c) a locksmithing kit

##### Multiclassing

The following contains information necessary for multiclassing as a Street Fighter.

**Ability Score Minimum.** You require both a Strength score and Constitution score of 13 or higher to multiclass into or out of this class.

If street fighter isn't your initial class, you gain the following proficiencies when you take your first level as a street fighter:

- **Armor.** Light armor
- **Weapons.** Improvised weapons

### Street Tough

Beginning at 1st level, while you are no armor and not wielding a shield, your AC equals 13 + your Constitution modifier. While you are wearing light armor, you use your Constitution instead of Dexterity to determine your additional AC.

### These Hands

Beginning at 1st level, your years of fighting in back alleys, bars and the streets have given you mastery over combat styles that use unarmed strikes and street fighter weapons, which are simple melee weapons without the two-handed property, whips, and improvised weapons. You can’t use the finesse property of a weapon while using it as a street fighter weapon.

You gain the following benefits while you are unarmed or using only street fighter weapons and you are wearing light or no armor and not using a shield:

- You can roll a d6 in place of the normal damage of your unarmed strike or street fighter weapon. This die changes as you gain street fighter levels, as shown in the These Hands column on the Street Fighter table.
- When you use the Attack action on your turn and make only unarmed strikes, attacks with street fighter weapons, shoves, or grapples, you can use a bonus action to make one grapple or unarmed strike.

### Mettle

Beginning at 2nd level, your vast experience on the streets has helped you develop the mettle of a champion fighter. This is represented by a number of mettle points. Your street fighter level determines the maximum number of points you have, as shown in the Mettle Points column of the Street Fighter table.

You can spend these points to fuel various mettle features. You start knowing three such features: Square Up, Basic Combination, and Hit and Run. You learn more mettle features as you gain levels in this class. You regain all expended mettle points when you finish a short or long rest.

- **Square Up.** You can use a bonus action and spend 1 mettle point to brace for attacks. You gain a number of temporary hit points equal to a roll of your hands die + your street fighter level + your Constitution modifier. You lose any remaining temporary hit points gained in this way after 1 minute.
- **Basic Combination.** Immediately after you take the Attack action on your turn, you can spend 1 mettle point to make two unarmed strikes as a bonus action.
- **Hit and Run.** You can use a bonus action and expend 1 mettle point to attempt to shove a creature or take the Dash action.

### Grin and Bear It

Beginning at 2nd level, whenever you are subject to an effect that both deals damage and has additional secondary effects on a failed save (such as the shockwave tech), you can willingly choose to fail the save. In doing so, you take damage as normal, regain one mettle point, and ignore all other secondary effects (such as forced movement, effects such as the frightened condition, etc.). You can't use this feature again until you finish a long rest.

You can use this feature twice between long rests starting at 8th level, three times between long rests starting at 13th level, and four times between long rests starting at 18th level.

### Just Getting Started

Beginning at 3rd level, when you take damage that reduces you to half your maximum hit points or less, you can use your reaction to gain temporary hit points equal to three times your street fighter level and regain all your expended mettle points. Once you use this feature, you can’t use it again until you finish a short or long rest.

### Fighting Discipline

Beginning at 3rd level, you choose a discipline that best exemplifies your style. Your discipline grants you features at 3rd level and again at 6th, 11th, and 17th level.

- [Bare Knuckle](/streetfighter:bare-knuckle)
- [Catch Wrestler](/streetfighter:catch-wrestler)
- [Dirty Dog](/streetfighter:dirty-dog)
- [Lucha Libre](/streetfighter:lucha-libre)
- [PACT Bouncer](/streetfighter:pact-bouncer)
- [Street Detective](/streetfighter:street-detective)

### Ability Score Improvement

When you reach 4th level, and again at 8th, 12th, 16th, and 19th level, you can increase one ability score of your choice by 2, or you can increase two ability scores of your choice by 1. As normal, you can't increase an ability score above 20 using this feature.

### Fight or Flight

Beginning at 4th level, you discover a strength inside you that can’t be broken. As a bonus action, you gain resistance to bludgeoning, piercing, and slashing damage for one minute. At the end of that minute, you gain a level of [physical exhaustion](/con:exhaustion), or fatigue.

### Extra Attack

Beginning at 5th level, you can attack twice, instead of once, whenever you take the Attack action on your turn.

### Downright Fierce

Beginning at 5th level, when you make an attack, you can declare you are taking a big swing. When you do, you have disadvantage on the attack roll and, if you hit with a street fighter weapon or unarmed strike, you deal the maximum result of your weapon’s damage die instead of rolling.

### Power of Mettle

Beginning at 6th level, your unarmed strikes count as technical for the purpose of overcoming resistance and immunity to non-technical attacks and damage.

### Footsies

At 7th level, you gain proficiency in Dexterity saving throws.

### Snap Out of It

Beginning at 7th level, you can use your action to end one effect on yourself that is causing you to be charmed or frightened.

### Momentum Shift

Beginning at 9th level, when you use your Just Getting Started feature, you can choose to also use this feature. If you do, you add your proficiency bonus to the damage of your unarmed strikes and street fighter weapons for the next minute.

Once you use this feature, you can’t use it again until you finish a long rest.

### Keep It Together

By 10th level, you have developed a natural knack for staying awake and standing in many situations where others would go down. You have resistance to software damage and have advantage on saving throws against effects that would make you stunned or unconscious.

### Unbreakable

Starting at 14th level, you gain advantage on Strength, Dexterity, and Constitution saving throws. Additionally, whenever you make a saving throw and fail, you can spend 1 mettle point to reroll it and take the second result.

### Tireless Athleticism

Starting at 15th level, your carrying capacity is doubled, and when you deal damage with a melee weapon or unarmed strike to an inanimate object, that damage is doubled. Your standing jump distance is the same as your running start jump distance.

### Get Back Up

Starting at 18th level, when you are reduced to 0 hit points, you can find a well of strength within you to draw from against all odds. When you do, you regain half of your maximum hit points, half of your maximum mettle points, and do not suffer the effects of any levels of exhaustion you have for the next 10 minutes. At the end of those 10 minutes, you gain a level of exhaustion. Once you use this feature, you can’t use it again until you finish a long rest.

### Master of the Streets

At 20th level, your Strength and Constitution ability scores increase by 2, and your Strength and Constitution maximum ability scores also increase by 2. Additionally, when you take a long rest, you recover 2 levels of exhaustion instead of 1 and you regain all your expended Hit Dice instead of only half.
