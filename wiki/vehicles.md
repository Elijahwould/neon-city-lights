Neon City is replete with vehicles of all shapes and sizes. Although there are many different kinds of them, they are mostly all subject to the whims of the Self-Drive Network (SDN), which allows anyone in the city to simply punch in their destination and allow autopilot to take control.

You see, Neon City's streets can be chockful of dangers, excitement, drive-by shootings and the increasingly common fucking while driving. Due to this, drivers in Neon City frankly don't always have the time or wherewithal to pay attention to mundane things. Such as, you know… driving. [Autobae Transport Services](/corp:autobae) provides a solution that is readily sold to the city: the now ever-present Self-Drive Network. By default, all vehicles, such as your sedan or motorcycle, must be connected to the network. One can happily drive their vehicle as they please, but natural limitations are made (such as preventing one from going far over the speed limit except for passing situations), and violations are easier to track. It is much easier, then, to relinquish control, allowing the SDN chip to take your car merrily down the road on its own. All vehicles have both the computer and sensory equipment on it powerful enough to handle this, allowing you, the rider, the freedom to let the vehicle do its thing.

This means that gone are the days where self-driving cars were monopolized by the richest. However, so are the days where you could just drive your vehicle. Obviously, not every vehicle will be used within Neon City proper. Turning off the SDN connection is not illegal outside of the Neon City limits. However, disconnecting from the network while in Neon City is a punishable crime.

It's not like the corpo cops would ever go after someone toting around multiple firearms, nor bother someone with more credits than a high-level executive knows what to do with. As with anything in Neon City, the downtrodden and destitute are the ones most impacted by this law at all.

# How Vehicles Work

A vehicle's statistics function like those for creatures, but they have additional considerations.

## Creature and Cargo Capacity

Creature capacity describes how many creatures can ride in the vehicle comfortably. More creatures can fit by squeezing or by clinging to the outside of the vehicle, though this is precarious. No more than twice as many creatures can cling to or squeeze into a vehicle. Creatures squeezing into a vehicle suffer disadvantage on all ability checks, attack rolls, and saving throws due to discomfort and limited mobility. A creature clinging to the outside must use their bonus action each round to hold onto the vehicle, and if they or the vehicle are damaged they must attempt a Dexterity saving throw (DC equal to damage dealt) or fall off.

Cargo capacity specifies how much cargo the vehicle can carry in addition to any creatures, usually in some sort of compartment specifically designed for cargo.

## Armor Class

Most vehicles are typically made of modern alloys and have an Armor Class of 16 + its Dexterity modifier. While a vehicle is stationary, attack rolls against it have advantage.

## Ability Scores

A vehicle has the six ability scores and corresponding modifiers. Its size and weight determines its Strength. Dexterity represents its handling and maneuverability. A vehicle’s Constitution reflects its durability and quality of construction. Vehicles usually have a score of 0 in Intelligence, Wisdom, and Charisma, as they are objects.

If a vehicle has a 0 in a score, it automatically fails any ability check or saving throw that uses that score.

## Hit Points

A vehicle’s hit points can be restored by making repairs to the vehicle (see Repairs, below). When a vehicle drops to 0 hit points, it ceases to function and is damaged beyond repair.

## Damage Threshold

Vehicles have bulk or armor that allows them to shrug off minor hits. A vehicle with a damage threshold has immunity to all damage unless it takes an amount of damage equal to or greater than its damage threshold value, in which case it takes damage as normal. Any damage that fails to meet or exceed the vehicle’s damage threshold is considered superficial and doesn’t reduce the vehicle’s hit points.

## Mishap Threshold

If a vehicle takes damage from a single source equal to or greater than its mishap threshold, it must roll on the Mishaps table.

## Vehicle Speed

A vehicle’s speed is listed in two parts. The first is its Combat Speed, and the second is its Travel Speed.

### Combat Speed

Most runner and corporate-owned vehicles have something of an internal switch. Even with top shelf models, there's no telling who has had their fingers in who's tailpipes, and just as many people want these things going fast as fuck as they do a steady shot from their chopper. As such, regardless of connection to the city's driving grid or lack thereof, these vehicles are fitted with rudimentary programming to allow for an inherent gearshift that recognizes the throes of combat from basic adrenaline scans, context sensitive audio-scenario restructuring, and driver speed of thought. When such a switch is flipped, the vehicle drastically slows down to course correct and prevent crashes, while providing access to its full suite of violence assisting options.

A vehicle’s combat speed is the speed at which a vehicle can safely be used as a platform in combat, be it for engaging at range with ranged weapons or in melee. While a vehicle may be able to go over the listed speed with its travel speed or by dashing, doing so in combat imposes disadvantage on checks to use melee or ranged weapons and tech, as well as checks involving movement between two vehicles. Modifications that modify speed increase the safe combat speed.

### Cruising Speed

A vehicle’s cruising speed is the base maximum speed that a vehicle can maintain while traveling, racing, or engaging in any other scenario besides combat. Some modifications may make this faster. Some modifications will increase a vehicle's maximum travel speed.

As a note, no ground vehicle can maintain over 350mph in the new world, with very few aerial vehicles topping out at 500mph.

### Acceleration and Deceleration

Anytime a vehicle travels its speed in a line, it is considered as accelerating.

An accelerating vehicle increases its speed on its next turn.

The Vehicle can Dash without gaining exhaustion in a Chase (See Chase rules below)

The Driver has Advantage for checks while moving in a straight line.

The Driver has Disadvantage for checks to maneuver the vehicle: Turn, Avoid a Crash, etc.

To decelerate, the Driver must spend the turn decelerating the car, and do no other action.
