## “The Web as I envisaged it, we have not seen it yet. The future is still so much bigger than the past.”

"Netscape" is a catch-all term that encompasses the whole of information, digital infrastructure, mass data servers, and power grids within or connected to Neon City. It can be most accurately thought of as an invisible sphere of technological influence enveloping the whole of the city, with various tethered satellites of influence, divided into two parts. There's the Materialplace, the physical realms, which constitute the whole of Neon City, the Undercity, and the Outskirts. And there's the Etherealnet, the digital realms, which in and of itself contains various super- and sub- realms and makes possible the many non-physical phenomena that advance tech unto the illusion of magic.

Technically, the Etherealnet is but one of few powerful digital realms which facilitate the life even a luddite of a renegade has long grown accustomed to. As the largest of the digital realms, the Etherealnet is attributed this title regardless, as it was the first of these realms to reach such a size and level of permanence to be denoted instead as a domain and is now foundational to the operation of the others.

People, subjectively, spend the majority of their lives online, where the advent of psychosomatic perspective warping can turn a day of real time spent inside the Netscape, either physically or projecting through a sense-tank, into a span of time as long as a year.

A great many things are possible online: adventures are made real in MMO subrealms, illicit substances and practices made safe in back alley subrealms, every corporate function made all the more entertaining and comfortable in social home subrealms.

A great many things that are possible, however, are not immediately evident or accessible to the common man. Every software owes some facet of its birth to the Netscape to which it will be anchored to. Paranoid techwiz's and hackers are rumored to hide in self-referencing subrealms making every contingency reality in a realm one-one millionth the pace of the physical realms. And in a realm of their own creation and control, corpo admins can play in auto-generated pantheons of their design, wearing the godhood they tout in their boardrooms.

So, care to log on?

# Structure of the Netscape

When talking about the netscape at large, it is important to understand the terminology. The following is a breakdown of each kind of category of space within the netscape proper.

## Realm

A realm is any digital area. While the locations depicted or replicated within might be tied or referenced from physical locations, the realm itself does not exist in a conventionally physical sense.

## Subrealm

A subrealm is a localized area of influence within a realm, typically created by the acquisition of admin rights to a certain server. Subrealms created by the selfsame tech are hosted in the Etherealnet.

## Superrealm

A superrealm is a greater extrapolation of digital influence. As realms have no greater limit on data capacity, the term is not used in relation to size, and instead pertains to long range data packet transfer to allow for digital and technical functionality between distant locations, such as the rainforest.

## Domain

A domain is a permanent fixture within the shifting landscape of realms, typically denoted by a specific form or function within the greater Netscape. There are five of them, and streetslang speakers know the domains as the corpo net, the dark web, the digital wastes, the pop-ups, and somewhat erroneously, the digital realms.

# Domains of the Netscape

There are five major domains in the Netscape. From the more general and easily accessible Etherealnet, to the dangerous and endlessly deteriorating Deprecated Overflow, each domain brings with it its own rules (or lack thereof), aesthetics and expectations. People that choose to navigate any of the domains of the Netscape should keep in mind what exactly they're getting themselves into.

### Etherealnet

Known as the Digital Realms in streetslang—a somewhat erroneous term—the etherealnet subsumes everything. By default, the etherealnet is a digital echo of the physical structure of the world, although it can be changed and manipulated by people with sufficient admin access. It is most often changed to host blaring advertisements and subrealm redirections to whatever local power has control over the digital area.

### Securenet

Streetslang derisively refers to this domain as the Corponet, and it's an accurate assessment. A massive series of interconnected realms and subrealms that were bought up, to the point of allowing disparate corporate interest to own a massive part of the Netscape's structure. The admin rights to sections of the securenet are constantly trading hands in double-blind buyoffs that keep any single corpo from owning the majority of it. Despite that, it remains a host for massive digital power and, as its name suggests, a secure place for online corporate interest.

Aesthetically, the securenet is completely beholden to the corporation that owns the section one resides in. It is the idealization of their ethos made manifest in a digital space.

### Chaosnet

Imagine if the Undercity had no physical boundaries, yet managed to find a way to be more cramped. Lovingly referred to in street slang as the Dark Web, the Chaosnet exists as a macro-scale extranet venture. The servers that maintain it, the satellites that connect it—all of it is constantly shifting. Its admin rights are not bought off; they are community owned—typically by server maintenance crews down in the Undercity, but not exclusively.

If the Securenet is the city's power in digital form, the Chaosnet is the city's information in digital form. Most corporate interests would tell you these are the same things, which is why the Dark Web's ability to shift and change is pivotal to its survival.

Aesthetically speaking, the Chaosnet is a curious place. The lax user permissions make it so that everyone's opinion and local profile affects their immediate surroundings. Basically? Imagine if r/place had survived to the current day—but with your thoughts!

### Deprecated Overflow

The Digital Wastes. Coined as such for the fact that manifesting within it looks for all the world like a vast silver desert under a starless sky, with cables, data structures and trickles of silver sand falling from nowhere. The overflow is where deleted files go when they die: the recycle bin of digital reality.

Despite the tumultuous nature of the overflow creating malware, it serves a necessary function to the life cycle of the Netscape. Theoretically, the space of the Netscape is finite, and while no human would ever find those limits… **every** human just might. And so, space that is no longer being used is powderized into the waste of the overflow, later to be recycled into other digital structures.

### Recursive Instances

The pop-ups—and just as annoying as they sound. On a positive note, recursive instances are just that: when a subrealm is overpopulated, an exact duplicate is created in the same digital space. That way, maximum occupancy can still be met. More clever users that are familiar with MMO video games might recognize the practice as "instancing".

On a more… negative note, some entities abuse this feature of the Netscape to flood the Etherealnet with what *they* want to exist.

Ironically, while the pop-ups represent more material to be churned into the digital wastes, it is theorized that if the overflow were the grinding of gears, pop-ups would be the sparks from the interaction. As such, they are actually more prevalent in areas that lead to the digital wastes.

Aesthetically, recursive instances can either look like an exact duplicate of the area they're copying, or like a duplicate of it put through 20 headache-inducing filters.█
