## "The things we do to the people that we love…"

During the relic wars, there was a significant leap in progress in the development of weapons, and techs, new remakes were made better, new weapons were being supplied to soldiers, new techs were being flung at each other. However, not all the individuals participating in the relic wars had access to the newest old-world discoveries. Some were still working with outdated chassis, some with outdated weapons. A group of individuals eventually found a set of remake chassis from the old world, perfectly suited for customization. Inserting newer power supplies and then pushing these chassis to their limits, they were able to push them further than any other soldiers on the battlefield.

In a roundabout way, they'd rediscovered the old-world practice of **overclocking**, and it made them a force to be reckoned with. Sadly, these are no more than rumors spouted by old, batshit crazy Remakes who fought in the Relic Wars, so who really knows if their memory banks can be trusted?

Right?

Nearly a hundred and fifty years ago, the Reliquary caught wind of this technology, and put it on their list of dangerous technologies, making it a priority to be contained if ever re-discovered. And, lo and behold, fifty years after that, the technology was discovered, and contained.

And that was the end of it.

At least, of course, until a member of [the Reliquary](/corp:reliquary) decided to steal the technology for themselves.

### Rediscovery

A geneticist by the name of *Leonardo Blihovde*, an Elf who was once a worker for [R.E.D. Genetics](/corp:red-genetics) and was under contract for the Reliquary, started a project around fifty years ago. His idea: this technology—once used to bring remakes with little to no modifications in line with the highest end combat models—could be used on *humans*, instead. With a firm grasp of biochemistry, and the knowledge of this forbidden technology stored in his eidetic memory, he started working.

The work was difficult, but not the most difficult part. The difficult part was finding a sample of people who wouldn't be missed should the program fail. Junkies, runners, criminals, the general unsavory types within Neon City, looking to make a quick buck, who aren't willing to put their own flesh on the line.

Dr. Blihovde was a ruthless individual.

The program had a success rate lower than 1%.

Of the original five hundred or so participants, only three survived.

The result of his experimentation was the creation of a drug, known as *Aion*. Named after the Hellenic god of Time, Aion is a perception-altering substance that makes the user perceive time at roughly 1/10th the rate. The finalized product now successful, this drug eventually went to market, becoming a recreational drug used by elfs, and high-tier business executives, for its highly addictive ability to increase productivity over the course of an extended span of time.

There were some rather nasty side effects, however. Those who found themselves overdosing on the substance would be rendered comatose, with no amount of intervention being able to lift them from their comatose state.

An Elf woman who was converted into a Remake after undergoing the engramization process—an attempt to rescue her after 10 years of failed attempts at waking her up—was interviewed on the nature of her coma, an experience she was able to recount in accurate, horrifyingly excruciating detail.

Stuck in her own body, her mind still active, but her body not moving. The dilation between perception and reality cranked too far into the red, her flesh having become her jail.

A hundred years' worth of perception, locked in place, moving slowly.

It is a wonder that she somehow managed to not break.

This side effect led to Aion being inevitably pulled from the market. In the end, it became accessible only through black market trades and direct manufacture, though very few were able to reproduce it in a way that didn't cause near-instant brain death.

### Resurgence

Within the past ten years, rumors have been stirring about a program that seeks to revive the original purpose of the program, which was never to create a drug for the elite. It was developed to create a corps of soldiers who would be able to move faster than perception, with the ability to perform complex calculations in their head at a rate unmatched by any other flesh-bound offshoot of humanity, only comparable to a computer.

Though Leonardo himself supposedly died in 1602 in a boating accident off the coast of Neon City, his research has been picked up by groups interested in using it to create these new soldiers. As a result, several have begun to pop up recently. While nobody knows quite where the black site for the trials is, they know that an unusual amount of Aion has been seen on the market, larger than any typical third-party producer would be able to manufacture.

Additionally, there seem to be a new set of speedware augmentations that have been released on the market: an autoinjector kit, only marked with a broken clock, that requires installation over the spine.

And they only seem to take the vials of Aion that have been found on the market recently, allowing for anyone to administer Aion as a stimulant, rather than a recreational drug, with calibrated systems to provide optimal dosages with no risk of overdose.
